Retiring computers at a law firm

Old laptops and desktops at a small firm usually still hold client files, email archives and drafts. Here's how the confidentiality rule applies when they retire. A plain-language summary, not legal advice.

The rule that applies

Rule 1.6(d) of the Pennsylvania Rules of Professional Conduct asks lawyers to make reasonable efforts to prevent unauthorized access to client information. Retired computers are easy to overlook: a laptop sitting in a closet, or one handed to a recycler without a record, can still hold everything that was on it. There's no Pennsylvania ethics opinion specifically about disposing of computers, so "reasonable efforts" is the standard to work from.

What reasonable looks like in practice

  • Find the local copies first. Documents saved to the desktop, email archive files and old downloads often live only on the machine, outside your document management system.
  • Destroy or wipe every drive, including spare drives, USB sticks and external backup drives in drawers.
  • Keep a record that would satisfy you if asked: each machine by serial number, who took it, and a certificate listing each drive and how it was handled. What the certificate should show.
  • Release the machines from device management and sign out of accounts before they go. The full checklist.

Who should handle it

Ask any vendor who actually destroys the drives, whether you'll get a record by serial number, and whether your firm's name will appear anywhere afterward. If your firm's own policy requires a certified disposal vendor, use one.

Source: Pennsylvania Rules of Professional Conduct, Rule 1.6. Checked November 2026. A general summary, not legal advice.
