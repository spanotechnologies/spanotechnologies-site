Getting rid of old office computers in Philadelphia.

They can't go in the trash, and the city's drop-off centers don't take business waste. Here are the options an office actually has, and what to ask before you choose one.

Why not the trash, or the city's drop-off?

Since January 24, 2013, Pennsylvania's Covered Device Recycling Act has banned computers, laptops, monitors, tablets and other covered electronics from household and business trash. What the law covers.

Philadelphia's Sanitation Convenience Centers are for residents only. The city says waste from businesses isn't accepted, and you'll be asked for proof of Philadelphia residency.

The options

Four ways out of the office.

Free business pickup

For three or more laptops or desktops in Philadelphia, the Main Line and Delaware County, we pick up for free, have every drive shredded by an R2v3-certified recycler, and send a certificate listing each drive by serial number. How it works.

Donate them

For 15 or more usable computers, PCs for People, the nonprofit partner in the city's PHLDonateTech program, offers free business pickup.

A certified recycler

Electronics recyclers take drop-offs and run paid pickups. Ask for an R2v3 or e-Stewards certified one, and ask how drives are handled before you hand them over.

Return the leased ones

Leased machines go back to the leasing company. Check the lease before anyone else takes them, since unreturned equipment is usually billed.

Five questions to ask whoever takes them

  1. What happens to the drives, and who does it? Ask whether they're shredded, erased, or both, and by whom.
  2. Will I get a record by serial number? A list of every machine and every drive is what lets you close them out of your asset records. What a certificate should show.
  3. Do I sign anything at pickup? A signed transfer listing each machine shows exactly what left the office and when.
  4. What happens to machines that still work? Resold, donated or recycled, it's worth knowing which, and that your company's name won't appear anywhere.
  5. Is there a minimum, or a fee? Free programs usually have a minimum count. Paid ones often charge per device or per pickup.

Before anyone comes

Back up what you need, and release the machines from device management, or they lock themselves to your company when they're reset. The full checklist.
