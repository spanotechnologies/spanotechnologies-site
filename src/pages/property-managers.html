How it works

One day, every tenant covered.

  1. Pick a day

    A weekday late afternoon or a Saturday morning, and a spot like a loading dock or a spare room. We send you a short notice to forward to tenants.

  2. Tenants bring them down

    Laptops, desktops and all-in-ones, any brand, working or not. Each tenant signs their own transfer form listing their machines by serial number.

  3. Each tenant gets a certificate

    Drives are shredded by an R2v3-certified recycler. Within 15 business days, every tenant gets a certificate listing their own drives by serial number, and nobody else's.

Why buildings do it

An easy thing to offer tenants.

Nothing to pay

Free for tenants and for the building when three or more computers come in. We refurbish and resell what can be reused, and that pays for the day.

Out of the trash

In Pennsylvania, computers can't go in the trash, and Philadelphia's drop-off centers don't take business waste. What the law covers.

Each tenant's data stays theirs

Every tenant's machines are logged on their own form and their drives listed on their own certificate. No tenant sees another's paperwork, and the recycler never sees a tenant's name.

Questions

Which buildings can you do?

Office buildings and coworking spaces in Philadelphia, the Main Line and Delaware County.

Do tenants have to sign anything?

Yes. Each tenant signs a short transfer of ownership listing their machines, and keeps a copy. That's what lets us issue a certificate in their name.

Can tenants get paid for newer machines?

Yes. A tenant with newer business laptops or desktops can send us a list beforehand and get a per-unit offer in writing, paid on the day.

Are there tenants you can't take?

Medical, dental and other offices holding patient records. Their equipment needs a different agreement, which we don't offer today.

How are the drives handled?

Removed after the collection, kept locked until our R2v3-certified recycler shreds them, and listed by serial number on each tenant's certificate. Every step is in our Data Destruction and Chain-of-Custody Policy.
