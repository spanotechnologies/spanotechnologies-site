Guide · Pennsylvania

Pennsylvania's electronics disposal law, for businesses.

What can't go in the trash, who has to arrange recycling, and which rules reach the data on old computers. A plain-language summary, not legal advice.

The trash ban

Pennsylvania's Covered Device Recycling Act (Act 108 of 2010) has banned covered devices from municipal trash since January 24, 2013. That applies to businesses as well as households, and haulers can refuse to collect them.

Covered devices are desktop and laptop computers, computer monitors, tablets and e-readers, televisions, and computer peripherals such as keyboards and printers.

Who arranges the recycling

The Act's free collection programs, run by device manufacturers, serve households and small businesses with fewer than 50 employees. Businesses with 50 or more employees, and public entities, have to make their own recycling arrangements.

Either way, the law is about where the devices go. It says nothing about the data on them.

The rules that reach the data

Pennsylvania has no general law on how private businesses dispose of data. Some rules still cover the information on retired computers, depending on the business:

  • Tax preparers and accounting firms. Under the FTC Safeguards Rule, a written security plan has to cover secure disposal of client data, and the IRS specifically mentions wiping or destroying old hard drives.
  • Law firms. Rule 1.6(d) of the Pennsylvania Rules of Professional Conduct asks lawyers to make reasonable efforts to prevent unauthorized access to client information. Retired computers are easy to overlook.
  • Anyone holding consumer reports. If you run employee background checks, the FTC Disposal Rule asks for reasonable measures when disposing of that information, and it names computer equipment.
  • Medical and dental offices. Patient records fall under separate federal rules. We don't take equipment from healthcare offices.

What this means in practice

Send retired computers to a recycler or a pickup service, never the dumpster. Make sure the drives are wiped or destroyed before the machines leave your control, or by someone who'll put it in writing. Keep that record with your asset list. What the record should show.
