Free business computer pickup

How it works

  1. Send a list

    How many, the makes and models, and about how old. We reply with a pickup time and, for newer machines, a per-unit offer in writing.

  2. We pick up

    Each machine is labeled and logged by serial number on a transfer of ownership we both sign, and we take everything with us.

  3. Drives are shredded

    Each drive is removed, recorded and kept locked until our R2v3-certified recycler shreds it. Your certificate follows within 15 business days. See a sample.

What we take

Three or more computers

Laptops, desktops and all-in-ones, any brand, working or not. Old and broken machines are fine: what can't be reused goes to a certified electronics recycler, not a landfill.

Cash for newer machines

Business laptops and desktops from roughly the last five years, Apple silicon Macs, and recent iPhones and iPads can get a per-unit offer. Everything else is a free pickup.

Before we come

Locked machines can't be reused, so these make the biggest difference to what we can offer. The full checklist.

Back up your data

Copy anything you want to keep. Once a drive is shredded, nothing on it can be returned.

Release device management

Remove the machines from Windows Autopilot and Intune, Apple Business Manager, and Absolute, or whatever tool your IT provider uses.

Remove BIOS passwords

Or tell us the password, so the machine can be reset and reused.

Gather the chargers

If you still have them. They help a machine find its next owner.

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes, for three or more computers in our area, including shredding the drives and recycling anything that can't be reused. Reselling the machines that still work pays for it.

Where do you pick up?

Philadelphia, the Main Line and Delaware County. See the towns we cover. Pickups are early mornings, late afternoons, or Saturdays if your office is open.

What happens to the drives after pickup?

Each drive is removed, its serial number recorded, and kept in a locked case until we deliver it to our R2v3-certified recycler for shredding. Your certificate lists every drive by serial number with the recycler's shredding receipt. If your policy requires the recycler's own drive-by-drive serial record, tell us before the pickup; that's available for a small per-drive fee. Every step is in our Data Destruction and Chain-of-Custody Policy.

What else do you take?

Along with a pickup of three or more computers: phones and tablets, monitors, docks, chargers, keyboards, cables, small printers, and loose hard drives or USB sticks. Phones and tablets are erased with the maker's own reset, or shredded if they're locked or your policy requires it, and every one is listed on your certificate. Network equipment is recycled. Servers, ask us first. We don't take copiers, TVs, projectors or battery backup units.

What about MacBooks and built-in storage?

Storage that can't be removed is erased with the maker's own erase function, so the machine can be reused. If your policy requires physical destruction, tell us before the pickup and those devices go to the recycler for shredding instead.

How does payment work?

For machines that qualify, we email a per-unit offer before the pickup and pay it at the pickup. The amount is written on the transfer of ownership.

Are you a certified ITAD company?

No. We're a small business, not a certified disposal facility. If your policy requires a certified vendor, PCs for People, the nonprofit partner in the city's PHLDonateTech program, offers free business pickup for 15 or more usable computers.

Do you take equipment from healthcare offices?

Not yet. Medical, dental and other offices holding patient records need a different agreement, and we don't offer one today.

What happens to the machines?

Working machines are tested and resold through our eBay store, and some that don't fully work are sold as parts, labeled that way. Drives are shredded or erased first, asset tags come off, and your company's name never appears in a listing. The rest is recycled.
