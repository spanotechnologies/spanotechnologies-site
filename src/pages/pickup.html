How it works

Three steps, start to finish.

  1. Tell us what you have

    Send a rough list: how many, what make and model, and about how old. We reply with a pickup time and, for newer machines, a per-unit offer in writing before we come.

  2. We pick up

    We log each machine by serial number on a signed transfer form and take everything with us, so the visit is quick. Drives are removed afterward and kept locked until an R2v3-certified recycler shreds them. Built-in storage, like a MacBook's, is erased with the maker's own erase function, or shredded if your policy requires it.

  3. You get the paperwork

    A signed transfer of ownership at pickup, then a certificate of data destruction listing every drive by serial number, with the recycler's shredding receipt, within 15 business days. See a sample.

What we take

Built for small offices.

Three or more

Laptops, desktops and all-in-ones, any brand. Three computers is the minimum for a free pickup.

Working or not

Old, slow and broken machines are fine. Anything that can't be reused is recycled through a certified electronics recycler, not sent to a landfill.

Cash for newer machines

Business laptops and desktops from roughly the last five years, and Apple silicon Macs, can get a per-unit offer. Older machines are a free pickup.

Before we come

Four things to do first.

Locked machines can't be reused, so these make the biggest difference to what we can offer. The full checklist.

Back up your data

Copy anything you want to keep. Once a drive is shredded, nothing on it can be returned.

Release device management

Remove the machines from Windows Autopilot and Intune, Apple Business Manager, and Absolute, or whatever tool your IT provider uses.

Remove BIOS passwords

Or tell us the password, so the machine can be reset and reused.

Gather the chargers

If you still have them. They help a machine find its next owner.

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes, for three or more devices in our area. There is nothing to pay, including shredding the drives and recycling what can't be reused. We refurbish and resell what can be reused, and that pays for the pickup. It's also why newer machines can get a cash offer.

Where do you pick up?

Philadelphia, the Main Line and Delaware County, including Lower Merion, Radnor and Haverford Townships and Conshohocken. Pickups are early mornings, late afternoons, or Saturdays if your office is open.

What happens to the drives after pickup?

After the pickup, each drive is removed, its serial number recorded, and kept locked until we deliver it to our R2v3-certified recycler for shredding. Your certificate lists every drive by serial number with the recycler's shredding receipt. If your policy requires the recycler's own drive-by-drive serial record, tell us before the pickup; that's available for a small per-drive fee. Every step is written down in our Data Destruction and Chain-of-Custody Policy.

What about MacBooks and built-in storage?

Storage that can't be removed is erased with the maker's own erase function, so the machine can be reused. If your policy requires physical destruction, tell us before the pickup: those devices go to the recycler for shredding, and the receipt is listed on your certificate.

How does payment work?

For machines that qualify, we email a per-unit offer before the pickup and pay it at the pickup. The amount is written on the transfer of ownership.

Are you a certified ITAD company?

No. We're a small business, not a certified disposal facility. If your policy requires a certified vendor, PCs for People, the nonprofit partner in the city's PHLDonateTech program, offers free business pickup for 15 or more usable computers.

Do you take equipment from healthcare offices?

Not yet. Medical, dental and other offices holding patient records need a different agreement, and we don't offer one today.

What happens to the machines?

Working machines are tested and resold through our eBay store. Some that don't fully work are sold there as parts units, labeled that way. None carries any data from your company: drives are shredded or erased first, company asset tags and stickers come off, and your company's name never appears in a listing. The rest is recycled through an R2v3-certified electronics recycler.
