Buying guide

How to read our listings

Used electronics are sold in different states. Here is what our wording means, so you know what will be in the box before you buy.

Used or for parts

Used

Powered on and checked. It works as described, and any fault we found is named in the listing. Covered by 30-day returns.

For parts

Does not fully work, or could not be fully tested. Sold as-is with no returns. The title says it is for parts.

What's in the box

Every laptop listing has one. It says exactly what ships.

Barebones

No storage drive, no operating system and usually no charger. Most of our business laptops are sold this way. You add your own SSD and install Windows or Linux.

With drive

Some units include an SSD, and the listing gives its size. MacBooks always include their built-in storage.

Charger

Included only when the listing says so. If it is not listed, plan on supplying your own.

Laptop battery notes

For laptops we don't quote cycle counts or hours of runtime. iPhones and iPads list a battery health percentage instead, covered below.

Holds charge

The laptop ran on its battery during testing.

AC only

It runs on the charger, but the battery does not hold a charge.

Not tested

We could not check the battery. Assume it may need replacing.

iPhone and iPad checks

On top of the condition and what's included, every iPhone and iPad gets these checks.

Activation Lock off

The device is signed out of iCloud and Find My, so it will set up under your Apple ID.

Battery health

The battery health percentage from the device's own settings is stated in the listing.

Carrier and IMEI

We check the carrier lock and that the IMEI is clean, and the listing says whether it is unlocked.

Faults and memory

Faults go in the title

A significant fault, such as a bad screen or keyboard, is in the listing title, not buried in the description.

Memory

Used RAM is pulled from working machines. Open box means unused memory in opened retail packaging. Type, capacity and speed come from the label on the stick.
