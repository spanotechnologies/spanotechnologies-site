Before you retire company computers

Eight things to do before the machines leave the office. Most take a few minutes, and two of them decide whether a machine can ever be used again.

  1. Make a list

    How many, what make and model, and about how old. A rough list is enough for a quote. Serial numbers get recorded at pickup.

  2. Back up what you need

    Check for files saved only on the machine: the Desktop and Downloads folders, email archives, local accounting or design files. Once a drive is shredded, nothing on it can be recovered.

  3. Move software licenses

    Deactivate or sign out of software licensed per computer, so the seat can move to a new machine instead of being bought again.

04 and 05 · the two that matter most

Unlock them, or nobody can use them

A managed or locked machine re-locks itself to your company when it's reset, so it can only be recycled.

Release device management

Remove the machines from Windows Autopilot and Intune, Apple Business Manager, and Absolute, or whatever tool your IT provider uses. If someone else manages your IT, a short email to them usually covers it.

Turn off Activation Lock and Google locks

Sign out of the Apple Account on each Mac, iPhone and iPad, which turns off Find My, and remove the Google account from Android phones and tablets. A device that's still signed in can't be reactivated by anyone else.

  1. Remove BIOS passwords

    Or write them down for whoever takes the machines, so they can be reset.

  2. Gather chargers and docks

    If you still have them. They help a machine find its next owner.

  3. Don't bother wiping drives that will be shredded

    If the drives are going to a shredder, wiping them first only adds time. Ask your vendor which drives will be shredded and which erased.

After they're gone

Get it in writing. A signed transfer at pickup listing every machine, then a certificate listing every drive by serial number. What the certificate should show.

Close out your records. Mark the machines retired in your asset list, and cancel anything billed per device, such as antivirus, backup or remote-management licenses.
