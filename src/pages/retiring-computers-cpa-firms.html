Retiring computers at a CPA or tax firm

Old workstations at a tax practice usually hold years of returns, bank details and Social Security numbers. Here's what the rules say about getting rid of them, and what to keep on file. A plain-language summary, not legal or tax advice.

Your security plan has to cover disposal

Tax preparers are covered by the FTC Safeguards Rule. Under it, a firm's written information security program has to include secure disposal of customer information, and the rule's small-firm exemption (fewer than 5,000 consumers) does not remove that disposal requirement.

The IRS points tax professionals the same way: your written security plan has to cover secure disposal of client data, and the IRS specifically mentions wiping or destroying old hard drives. Firms that only do audit work may not be covered by the same rules, so check with your own advisor.

What that means when computers retire

  • Know what's leaving. List every machine by serial number before it goes, so it can come off your asset records.
  • Make sure the drives are destroyed or wiped, and by whom. Deleted files can usually be recovered from a drive that hasn't been.
  • Keep the paperwork with your security plan: a signed transfer of the equipment, and a certificate listing each drive by serial number and how it was handled. What the certificate should show.
  • Don't forget the rest: scanners and copiers with built-in storage, old backup drives and USB sticks in desk drawers.

Timing around tax season

January through April is the wrong time to swap equipment. Late spring, after busy season, or the fall is usually easier. Back up what you need first. The full checklist.
